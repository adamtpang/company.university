# AITX Autonomous Vehicle Hiring Hackathon

Idea only. Not scheduled, not sold, and not a commitment.

Company University's thesis is that every company gets a school: feeder programs that connect companies with people who are learning. This note is one possible feeder. A hiring hackathon in Austin, run with the Austin AI community.

## The concept

Run the hackathon with AITX, the Austin AI community. Co-founders: Michael Daigler and Jake O'Shea.

Autonomous vehicle companies sponsor real problems from their own work. Tesla, Waymo, Avride, and others are the first names to approach. Builders show up and work those problems. Winners get a job offer or a fast-tracked interview. What that prize actually is remains open. See below.

## Why it fits

A feeder is the product. The company does not sort a pile of cold applications. It watches people prove skill on a problem the company already has.

That is the academy bet: real work, watched, tied to a real hire. Demand comes first. The companies bring the problems. The hackathon is the proof, not a course that hopes a job shows up at the end.

Precedent: AITX already co-hosted the Base Power x AITX Talent Hackathon, September 25-27, 2026. The shape has been run once, a company plus the talent community. This idea repeats it for autonomous vehicles.

## Why autonomous vehicles, and why Austin

Austin is a testing hub for Tesla Robotaxi, Waymo, and Avride. Those companies need engineers who can ship. A short, public build on their actual problems is a tighter signal than a resume.

## Open questions

- Format: a weekend, or one day?
- Who judges, and against what rubric?
- What does a sponsor actually give: a real problem, engineers in the room, interview slots, a prize, or all of those?
- What does a "job offer" prize look like? Company University does not promise placement. The standing offer is a fast track into a final round, never a guaranteed hire. Write the prize that way before anyone is told otherwise.
- How does one event serve many companies, instead of being tailored to one? The current rule is one school for one employer's funnel, until a single hire loop is proven. A shared hackathon has to respect that, or say plainly why this case is different.
